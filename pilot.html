  • Outcome review before expansion

Pilot sequence

Move from workflow truth to measured proof.

The exact scope depends on the use case, data, integrations, and customer requirements. A typical sequence looks like this.

Define the workflow

Map the current path, participants, handoffs, failure points, controls, and the outcome the pilot must demonstrate.

Rehearse representative data

Profile a bounded source sample, define mappings and exceptions, load to a staging environment, and reconcile results before live use.

Configure the operating model

Set matter structure, roles, guidelines, rates, approval paths, budgets, reporting, and deployment requirements for the chosen workflow.

Onboard the participants

Prepare selected in-house users and firms with clear responsibilities, a support path, and acceptance criteria.

Run the workflow

Process real or controlled transactions end to end while monitoring data quality, turnaround, exceptions, and adoption.

Review the evidence

Compare results with the agreed baseline, document remaining gaps, and make an informed go, adjust, or stop decision.

Example measures

Success should be observable.

A pilot scorecard is tailored to the workflow; these are practical starting points.

Data

Migration confidence

Required-field completeness, mapping exceptions, rejected records, and source-to-target reconciliation.

Quality

First-pass readiness

Invoices or requests that arrive complete enough to proceed without client-side cleanup.

Time

Cycle improvement

Time from submission to a usable matter, resolved exception, approval, or AP-ready record.

Control

Review consistency

Application of rates, guidelines, budgets, roles, and approval rules across the pilot population.

Adoption

Participant completion

Internal and firm users who complete the intended workflow without an off-system workaround.

Finance

Close-the-loop accuracy

Allocation, AP export, payment status, accrual, and reconciliation results where included in scope.

A migration is not an upload button

Speed comes from bounding risk.

We do not promise that unknown legacy data will be clean or instantly portable. Pilot migration scope is based on an inspected sample, explicit mappings, exception handling, reconciliation totals, and rehearsal before cutover. The goal is a fast path you can defend—not a fast import you later have to unwind.

Good pilot fit

Specific enough to learn. Important enough to matter.

One recurring workflow

A defined invoice, intake, matter, budget, accrual, or outside counsel process with visible friction.

A bounded participant group

A small set of engaged internal users and, where relevant, selected firms or business requesters.

Representative records

Enough real-world variation to expose mappings and exceptions without making the first test a full migration.

A decision to make

Agreed measures and stakeholders who can decide whether to expand, adjust, or stop after the review.

Clear scope before kickoff

Know what the pilot includes.

Subscription, implementation, migration, integration, deployment, and support responsibilities are scoped explicitly before work begins. No surprise category should appear after the pilot starts.
